What is RinkLens for?

RinkLens is for hockey fans who trust what they see on the ice but want to understand what the advanced numbers are saying. We turn complicated NHL statistics into plain-English explanations, easy player comparisons, and transparent scores that show what the data tells us. You can also add your own Eye Test grades based on the games you watch, because numbers only tell part of the story. Everything is tied to the season you select, not a player’s entire career, so you can follow the game and talk hockey with more confidence.

ONE TRANSPARENT MONEYPUCK-BASED SCORE

How the Advanced Stats Score works

A simple 0–100 summary of five 5-on-5 advanced statistics plus total five-on-five minutes played. Four comparable percentage metrics use an equal MoneyPuck/Natural Stat Trick average when a verified, matching export has been imported; otherwise they stay MoneyPuck-only. Individual expected goals per 60 and ice time remain MoneyPuck-only. Forwards and defensemen use different weights and are compared only with their position group within the selected season. Scores require real five-on-five minutes and games. This is a descriptive RinkLens calculation, not a score provided by MoneyPuck.

MoneyPuck 5v5 metricForwardsDefensemen
Expected goals share (xGF%)35%40%
Individual expected goals / 60 (ixG/60)25%10%
Shot-attempt share (CF%)10%15%
Unblocked shot-attempt share (FF%)5%10%

Total five-on-five minutes played10%10%
Total100%100%

Why different weights? Forwards get more emphasis on individual expected-goal creation; defensemen get more emphasis on on-ice chance quality and shot shares. Total five-on-five minutes played carries 10% for both positions to account for workload and availability. CF% and FF% overlap, so their combined weight is limited. These are transparent editorial choices—not a validated all-around player-value model.

Eligibility: A player must have at least 10 five-on-five games and 100 minutes of five-on-five ice time. Early in the season (until the most games played by any skater reaches 15), the requirements are 3 five-on-five games and 30 five-on-five minutes. At least 30 qualified players at the same position group must have data for each included metric.

Calculation: Take the same-position, same-season percentile of each advanced metric and total five-on-five minutes, apply the corresponding weights, and average. Five-on-five minutes are a separate 10% component and cannot replace the requirement for four actual advanced metrics, including xGF% and ixG/60. If one optional metric is missing, rescale the remaining weights rather than treating it as zero. The result is adjusted toward 50 using effective five-on-five games / (effective games + 10); effective games is the lesser of actual five-on-five games and five-on-five minutes / 12. This reliability adjustment still uses games played and total five-on-five minutes, but only total five-on-five minutes have the explicit 10% scoring weight.

Reliability labels: Small sample: fewer than 20 five-on-five games or 300 five-on-five minutes. Moderate sample: fewer than 45 five-on-five games or 750 minutes. Larger sample: at least 45 games and 750 minutes. Labels describe sample size, not certainty that a player is good or bad.

Source transparency: If both sources have the same player's unadjusted, regular-season 5-on-5 xGF%, CF%, FF%, or HDCF%, their percentages are averaged 50/50. We don't average unlike measurements, and MoneyPuck remains the sole source for ixG/60, GP, ice time, and counting stats. Different expected-goals models may produce different numbers. The Advanced Stats Score stays separate from the fan Eye Test. Missing NST data never becomes an invented value.

STARTING FORMULA + REAL VIEWER GRADES

Eye Test

Every skater gets a statistics-linked automatic Eye Test ranking, using the original Formula Baseline (season games, average ice time, points/game, 5-on-5 goal differential) and restricting the displayed ranking to within 5 points of their Advanced Stats Score. Skaters below the normal advanced-stat minimum use a provisional score from the same five-on-five metrics, labeled as a small sample. This is an automated estimate, not an actual Eye Test observation. Signed-in viewers can grade eight position-specific skills from 1–10. Their grades influence the combined score; individual reviews affect only the reviewer's private preview until three community reviewers qualify. The qualified Advanced Stats Score calculation and its minimum sample requirements remain unchanged.

How the automatic Eye Test ranking works

Automatic limit: Start with the original position-weighted Formula Baseline below. The displayed automatic Eye Test ranking must be no more than 5 points above or below the Advanced Stats Score. Example: Advanced Stats Score 70 → Auto Eye Test ranking from 65 through 75. For players without a qualified Advanced Stats Score, a clearly marked provisional 5-on-5 reference uses the same metrics and a strong small-sample adjustment. This is not an independent scouting grade. If even the provisional reference lacks sufficient data, no auto score is invented.

These are updated weights based on the other NHL Player Ranking project, applied to this season's skaters using same-position percentiles. We have added a 10% five-on-five goal differential component and reduced points-per-game weight by 10 percentage points. The original 100-career-game minimum is not applicable to a single season. Goalies are not included because this version of RinkLens contains MoneyPuck skaters only.

First, convert each input to its position-group percentile. Next, average using the stated weights, then draw early-season scores toward 50 with GP / (GP + 10). The 5-on-5 +/- figure counts goals for minus goals against while the player is on the ice at even-strength five-on-five. It is not the NHL’s official +/- and can be affected by teammates and goaltending. Where the source is missing, RinkLens redistributes the available formula weights rather than treating missing +/- as zero. The formula is a statistical estimate, not an observation of skating, decision-making or defensive skill.

Ratings: RinkLens and real fans

Every scored player starts with 1 RinkLens automated rating in the same ratings display as fan submissions, clearly marked as model-generated, not a human vote. This lets visitors see that RinkLens has evaluated the player even before any fans have reviewed them. Signed-in fans can then submit their own eight-skill grades. The site shows the model rating, real fan ratings or community average, and their contributions separately. RinkLens never submits a fake fan ballot, and its model rating does not count toward the three real reviewers needed for a public fan average.

Before reviews: the ranking is the bounded automated starting score. After your first grade: a private preview weighs the bounded starting score 70% and your actual grade 30%. After three public reviewers: the public ranking weighs the starting score 50% and the community average 50%; four reviewers contribute 60%, and five or more 70%. The final displayed ranking stays within ±5 of the advanced or provisional reference. The actual human grades remain visible separately at their full, unmodified values, even when they differ by more than 5 points.

Each player with a statistical rating has one labeled RinkLens model contribution; it is not a human submission. Current public community averages still require three distinct human reviewers. A single signed-in person's rating is displayed to that person privately, not publicly. More reviewers gradually give real fan observation more influence. This stat-anchored Eye Test ranking is deliberately linked to the Advanced Stats Score; the individual and community human grades remain distinct, unmodified opinions.

STAT DEFINITIONS

What do these numbers mean?

All-situations counting totals and 5-on-5 advanced metrics are kept separate.

Points / 60 (P/60)

Goals plus assists for every 60 minutes of the player's total ice time. All game situations.

Expected goals share (xGF%)

Share of expected goals generated by the team while the player is on the ice at 5-on-5. This reflects chance quality and is influenced by teammates and opponents.

Corsi (CF%)

Share of all shot attempts by the player's team while on the ice at 5-on-5. It does not directly measure time of possession.

Fenwick (FF%)

Share of unblocked shot attempts at 5-on-5.

Individual expected goals / 60 (ixG/60)

Expected goals generated by the player's own shots per 60 minutes of 5-on-5 ice time.

High-danger chance share (HDCF%)

Share of high-danger shots for versus against while the player is on ice at 5-on-5.

The Advanced Stats Score uses MoneyPuck, plus matched Natural Stat Trick percentages only when a permitted export has been imported; the Formula Baseline is still derived from MoneyPuck. Neither is an official source-published rating or subjective scout grade. The combined Eye Test is a clearly identified blend of the baseline and actual viewer grades when available. Small season samples may be volatile. A dash means required data is unavailable, not a zero rating. See MoneyPuck's glossary.
